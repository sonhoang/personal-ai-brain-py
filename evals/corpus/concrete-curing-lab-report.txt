LAB REPORT LR-0931: EFFECT OF CURING TEMPERATURE ON CONCRETE COMPRESSIVE STRENGTH

SYNTHETIC SAMPLE DOCUMENT. Written for the personal-ai-brain evaluation set.
The laboratory, project and results are invented.

Purpose

The bridge deck contractor asked whether the C30/37 mix can be poured in late autumn
without heated enclosures. We cast cylinders from one batch and cured them at three
temperatures to measure how curing temperature changes early and 28-day strength.

Method

Thirty-six cylinders (150 mm x 300 mm) were cast from a single batch with a
water-cement ratio of 0.45. Twelve were cured at 5 C, twelve at 12 C and twelve at
20 C, all at 95% relative humidity. Four cylinders from each group were crushed at
3 days, 7 days and 28 days according to EN 12390-3.

Results

Mean compressive strength in MPa:

At 20 C: 3 days 21.4, 7 days 31.0, 28 days 44.2.
At 12 C: 3 days 15.2, 7 days 25.8, 28 days 42.9.
At 5 C: 3 days 8.1, 7 days 17.6, 28 days 38.5.

The 5 C group reached only 57% of the 7-day strength of the 20 C group. At 28 days the
gap narrowed to 13%. All groups passed the 37 MPa characteristic cube strength
requirement at 28 days, but the 5 C group passed with a margin of only 1.5 MPa.

Discussion

Low curing temperature slows hydration, which mostly delays strength rather than
reducing it. The practical risk is early: formwork cannot be stripped and the deck cannot
take construction loads until the concrete reaches 20 MPa, which took about 3 days at
20 C but about 9 days at 5 C.

Recommendation

Pours with a forecast daily mean below 8 C should use insulated blankets for the first
7 days, or an accelerating admixture approved by the designer. Stripping should be based
on maturity sensors or field-cured cylinders, not on a fixed number of days.
